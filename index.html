Houses & Property

Forza Horizon 6 scatters 8 player houses across Japan, and each comes with a permanent perk that stays active no matter where you spawn. The headline is The Estate (Yashiki House) — a fully buildable plot you shape with an upgraded EventLab toolset. Houses unlock through the Discover Japan Stamp system (yellow → green → blue → orange → gold). Heads-up: the game also has 8 separate Garages (fast-travel + car-display points) — a different set of "8", not the houses. [RPS, TECH, FH6GUIDE]

Living in Japan

  • Unlock Mei's House first — it's free and opens car trading at the Autoshow.
  • The Estate costs just 10,000 Cr and is your permanent, buildable base; claim it early.
  • House perks stay active wherever you spawn, so owning all 8 stacks every bonus.
  • Most houses need a Discover Japan Stamp tier (yellow → gold) before they're buyable.
  • Hakusan Mountain Lodge gives the best ROI: an extra garage slot plus +10% Horizon Life credits.
  • Tokyo House's daily Wheelspin is the strongest long-term credit grind (free with VIP).
  • Garages are separate fast-travel points — unlock all 8 to cross the big map in seconds.
  • At The Estate, up to 12 players co-create via Horizon Collab; browse and download community layouts.

Encyclopedia

Wiki

Quick-reference encyclopedia for Horizon Japan. Systems and terminology that carry across the Forza Horizon series are described in general terms; FH6-specific facts are noted. Nothing here is invented — see the source notes.

Drivatars

The AI racers that fill your lobbies. They mimic the driving styles of real Forza players, so they brake, block, and recover more like humans than scripted bots.

Skill chains

Link drifts, jumps, near-misses and clean racing to build a Skill multiplier. A big chain banks Skill Points (SP) you spend in each car's Mastery tree.

Influence & Accolades

The festival's progression currency. Earning accolades unlocks wristbands, new events, and the path toward the Horizon Legend rank.

Wheelspins

Prize spins granted by level-ups, story events and the Festival Playlist. A Super Wheelspin is the rarer version with better odds at cars and credit bundles.

Forzathon

Rotating in-world events with their own point shop. Show up, complete the themed challenges, and spend Forzathon Points on cars, emotes and cosmetics.

Festival Playlist

The live content backbone: weekly and daily seasonal challenges. Completing them is the main way to earn exclusive cars and accolades.

Road Racing

Tarmac circuits and sprints. Favours grip, top speed and braking — the bread-and-butter of the festival.

Dirt Racing

Loose-surface stages through Japan's countryside. Look for rally-bred hatches and lightweight buggies.

Cross Country

Rugged point-to-point routes across fields, rivers and trails. Big, tough machinery wins here.

Street Racing

Illegal-feeling night Touge and city runs. Tight, technical, and all about rhythm and momentum.

Drag

Straight-line launches from a standing start. Brutal acceleration and launch control decide it.

Drift

Style over speed — hold the longest, smoothest slide for points. Differentials and tire setup matter most.

Performance Index (PI)

Every car is ranked by a single PI number so you can race fair. Upgrades push the number up; the class is just a band of that number.

D / C / B / A

The lower four classes. D is the most beginner-friendly; A is fast but still accessible for most events.

S1 / S2

Superbike-grade and hypercar territory. High power and aero; reserved for the toughest events and PR stunts.

X (Exceptional)

The unrestricted class for the absolute fastest, most heavily modified machines in the game.

Circuit & Sprint

Closed-loop and point-to-point races against Drivatars — the core of the campaign.

Speed Zones

Hit the highest average speed through a marked corridor. Reward Super Wheelspins and accolades.

Danger Signs

Launch off a ramp and fly as far as possible. A favourite for big-air screenshots.

Drift & Trailblazer

Score points in a slide, or blaze a ghost route as fast as you can. Both feed Skill Points.

Horizon Japan

FH6's setting: a fictionalised, festival-take on Japan with the largest open-world map in the series [WIKI].

Regions

Japan is split into regions — community guides report around ten, from Tokyo and Mount Fuji to rural towns and islands.

The Estate

Your customisable mountainside property in rural Japan. Build, decorate, and collect daily credit payouts [WIKI].

Garages

Eight garages double as fast-travel points and car-display showrooms across the map [WIKI].

Wristband

The rank badge you earn by clearing accolade milestones. Higher wristbands unlock more of the map and events.

Discover Japan stamps

Collectible stamps earned by exploring regions, finding landmarks and photographing them.

Mascots

Scattered collectibles you drive through for credits as you explore.

Barn Find / Treasure Car

Two kinds of hidden free cars: barn finds appear as stamp-gated rumours, treasure cars hide behind photo clues with no marker until found.

ANNA & Cinema Mode

ANNA is the in-car assistant that can drive you to a destination; Cinema Mode hides the UI for clean screenshot tours.

Guides

Step-by-step walkthroughs built from the real game systems and the confirmed FH6 details on this site. Where a count comes from community reporting rather than an official source, it's flagged.

Your first 10 hours

Roadmap

A relaxed path from the Qualifiers to a garage you actually like.

  1. 1

    Run the Qualifiers, then the Invitational

    Finishing the Horizon Invitational grants your first (Yellow) Wristband and unlocks the festival, multiplayer and free cars.

  2. 2

    Pick a starter that fits you

    Silvia K'S for drift, Celica GT-Four for balance, GMC Jimmy for off-road. All three end up in your garage anyway.

  3. 3

    Don't spend credits at the Autoshow

    Wheelspins, story events and the Festival Playlist hand out cars fast. Tune your starter instead.

  4. 4

    Photo the landmarks

    Press Up for photo mode. Landmark shots feed your Collection Journal and earn Discover Japan stamps.

  5. 5

    Three-star a few PR stunts

    Speed Zones, Danger Signs and Drift Zones pay Super Wheelspins and Skill Points for Car Mastery.

  6. 6

    Raise difficulty for credits

    Higher difficulty with assists off can pay up to +125% credits per race — once you can still win.

Unlock all 14 Barn Finds

Collectibles

Classic cars hidden in barns — gated by exploration, not luck.

  1. 1

    Earn Discover Japan stamps

    Explore regions, find landmarks and photograph them. Stamps are what unlock barn rumours.

  2. 2

    Watch for the rumour notification

    Once a region's stamps are met, a Barn Find rumour appears. You'll get a map hint rather than an exact pin.

  3. 3

    Drive to the hint and search

    Barns are tucked into the landscape. Roll up slowly and the find triggers automatically.

  4. 4

    Restore and enjoy

    Found barns go to your garage. 14 are reported across Japan — community-sourced; Wikipedia is silent.

Hunt the 9 Treasure Cars

Collectibles

Abandoned cars found only through photo clues — no marker until discovered.

  1. 1

    Get the photo clue

    Treasure clues show a snapshot of the location. Study the background — mountains, signs, road shape — to narrow it down.

  2. 2

    Match it on the map

    There is no map marker. Use landmarks and the horizon to triangulate the spot.

  3. 3

    Drive over the dig site

    Once you're close, the dig prompt appears. 9 treasure cars are reported — community-sourced; Wikipedia is silent.

Drift tuning basics

Tuning

Make almost any car slide predictably.

  1. 1

    Tires: lower the pressure

    Drop rear tire pressure a few PSI for a looser, more forgiving slide.

  2. 2

    Differential: bias to the rear

    Increase rear deceleration (and acceleration) so the back steps out on throttle lift and braking.

  3. 3

    Alignment: add caster

    More positive caster sharpens turn-in and self-centring, which keeps drifts controllable.

  4. 4

    Power & weight

    RWD with healthy power drifts best; a little ballast at the rear helps rotation. Then practice on the Touge.

Farm credits efficiently

Economy

Credits fund upgrades and the cars you actually want.

  1. 1

    Turn difficulty up

    Higher difficulty with assists off can pay up to +125% credits per race — only when you can still win.

  2. 2

    Three-star PR stunts

    Speed Zones, Danger Signs and Drift Zones award Super Wheelspins, which can pay out big credit bundles.

  3. 3

    Bank Wheelspins

    Level-ups, story events and the Festival Playlist all grant spins — save them and open in batches.

  4. 4

    Buy Aftermarket, not Autoshow

    Green CT icons on the minimap sell secondhand, fully-tuned cars cheaper than new.

Complete the 10 regions

Exploration

See everything Horizon Japan has to offer.

  1. 1

    Drive, don't fast-travel

    Explore first to fill the map; you can blink to visited spots later. Stamp every region.

  2. 2

    Clear PR stunts per region

    Each area has its own Speed Zones, Danger Signs and Drift Zones — great for accolades and spins.

  3. 3

    Collect mascots

    Drive through the scattered mascots for credits as you go.

  4. 4

    Photograph landmarks

    Landmark shots complete the Collection Journal and stack Discover Japan stamps.

Build the Estate

Property

Your own mountainside base in rural Japan [WIKI].

  1. 1

    Unlock it

    The Estate becomes available as you progress the festival in rural Japan.

  2. 2

    Build & decorate

    Place buildings and style the grounds to taste — it's your personal festival hub.

  3. 3

    Collect daily payouts

    Check the My Horizon message center for daily credit payouts from your property.
