Houses & the Estate

The Estate (Yashiki House) is your customisable mountainside property in rural Japan. Clear the 19 objects blocking the land and pay 10,000 Cr, and it becomes a permanent plot that friends can visit, with up to 12 players co-creating through Horizon Collab.

Houses are gated by Discover Japan stamps and credits. Mei's House is free and auto-unlocks after the tutorial with the Yellow stamp; the Tokyo House costs 3,000,000 Cr (free with VIP); the Vision House needs the Gold stamp (Master Explorer).

Nine categories, powered by an upgraded EventLab toolset: the main house, a garage wing with up to 50 display slots, a workshop, a viewing deck, driveways and paths, gardens and landscaping, decorative items, and seasonal changes that transform the plot with Japan's seasons.

Progression & live content

It is the live content backbone. A new season drops each week across a four-week Series, handing out exclusive cars, credits and accolades for daily and weekly challenges. We list reward types only — never a specific car we cannot verify.

A special area unlocked with the Gold Wristband, featuring unique challenges, exclusive events, and a second Horizon Festival [WIKI].

The Eliminator battle-royale mode supports up to 72 players [WIKI].

The world

A fictionalised Japan — the largest open-world map the series has ever built [WIKI]. Confirmed areas include Tokyo, Legend Island, rural Japan and Mount Fuji as a backdrop; community guides report the full map spans around ten regions.

Run the Horizon Qualifiers and the Horizon Invitational for your first wristband, pick a starter that matches your style, don't blow credits at the Autoshow, and unlock garages as fast-travel points as you explore. The 14-step Beginner's Guide walks through the whole path.

Features & Systems

New Gameplay Features

via Gamersky

What is new in Horizon Japan: seasons, Horizon Rush, Time Attack, Car Meets, CoLab, audio, and customization.

  • Japan setting with real landmarks (Shibuya Crossing, Ginkgo Avenue, Tokyo Tower), C1-loop-inspired roads, and passes like Haruna and Bandai-East.

Three Progression Systems

via Gamersky

The three core progression tracks: Wristbands, Stamps, and Horizon Play.

  • Wristbands: 7 total; your pass to stricter-class festival events. The gold wristband unlocks Legend Island and the series' longest Goliath race.
  • Stamps: 7 earned from the 'Explore Japan' storyline, inspired by Japanese stamp-collecting culture; fill the book to unlock garage treasure rumours, properties, and the grandparents' Estate.
  • Horizon Play: online modes (The Eliminator battle royale, Hide & Seek, Touge Showdown best-of-3, Spec Racing with stock cars) plus Free Race and Horizon Drift; independent level to 100 with badges, and series-resetting leaderboards.

The Eliminator (King of the Hill) Tips

via Gamersky

Core tips for the Eliminator battle-royale mode's duel mechanics.

  • Never duel at the circle center - fight at the edge and run toward the center; your duel endpoint spawns along the diameter toward the middle.
  • If the circle covers a main road (e.g. a highway), focus duels there - run it back and forth or camp both ends.
  • In the finals with a weak car, exit opposite to the mainstream direction and hug the 3-second exit limit.
  • When chasing, don't overcommit near the edge - veterans bait you out then steal the start. If you're being chased, crash into a restroom/small house to stop them and steal a big start.

Chinese Text + English Audio

via Gamersky

Keep the Chinese UI but switch the voice-over to English (or Japanese).

  • Set game language to Simplified Chinese, then in the install's /media/Stripped/StringTables folder swap the names of CHS.zip and EN.zip (so CHS.zip now holds the English audio).
  • In C:/Users/<you>/AppData/Local/ForzaHorizon6 create a file named 'UserPreferredLang' (no extension) containing 'EN' (or 'JP' for Japanese dub).
  • Game updates can revert the files - just repeat the steps.
